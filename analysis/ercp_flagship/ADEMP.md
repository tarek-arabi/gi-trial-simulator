# ADEMP protocol: designing the definitive trial of ERCP timing in acute cholangitis

This protocol is written **before** the simulation is run. It landed in the repository in the same
commit as the results, so the history does not independently evidence that ordering; see the caveat
in `REPORT.md`. It follows the ADEMP
structure of Morris, White and Crowther (Stat Med 2019;38(11):2074-2102, doi:10.1002/sim.8086):
Aims, Data-generating mechanisms, Estimands, Methods, Performance measures.

Parameter pack: `ercp_acute_cholangitis` v1.0.0. Every event rate below is a published aggregate
result, verified against its primary source on 2026-07-27. No restricted-use or patient-level data
enters this study.

---

## A — Aims

The clinical question is open. The largest randomised evidence is a single-centre trial of 304
patients with mild-to-moderate acute cholangitis (Jagtap et al., Gut 2026, doi:10.1136/gutjnl-2025-337279,
NCT05920954), whose primary endpoint of 30-day mortality gave 3.95 percent with urgent ERCP versus
6.58 percent with early ERCP, HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.25 to 1.93). That interval is compatible with both a
large benefit and a meaningful harm. An earlier meta-analysis of nine observational studies (Iqbal
et al., Gastrointest Endosc 2020;91(4):753-760.e4) reported in-hospital mortality OR 0.52 (95% CI
0.28 to 0.98) for ERCP within 48 hours, which is significant but observational and therefore
confounded by indication.

The aims of this simulation study are:

1. **A1.** Determine the sample size a definitive randomised trial would require to resolve the
   30-day mortality question at conventional error rates, across the range of treatment effects the
   existing evidence makes plausible.
2. **A2.** Compare three designs on their operating characteristics: a fixed design, a
   group-sequential design, and a Bayesian adaptive design with interim posterior-probability
   stopping rules. Specifically, quantify what each buys in expected sample size and what it costs
   in maximum sample size.
3. **A3.** Quantify how much a pre-specified prognostic covariate (a PROCOVA-style prognostic score)
   could reduce the required sample size, and confirm that such adjustment does not inflate the type
   I error rate.
4. **A4.** Establish whether the definitive trial is worth running in information terms, using
   expected value of sample information across a grid of candidate trial sizes.

A secondary aim, **A5**, is methodological rather than clinical: to demonstrate that every design
quantity reported here agrees with the established engines (rpact, gsDesign) wherever those engines
cover the case, so that the simulated quantities that have no closed form can be trusted by
extension.

## D — Data-generating mechanisms

Individual patients are generated as independent Bernoulli outcomes within arm, with 1:1 allocation.

**Primary endpoint.** All-cause mortality at 30 days.

**Control arm (early ERCP, 24 to 48 hours).** Event rate fixed at the anchoring trial's observed
0.0658. Because a single trial of 304 patients estimates that rate imprecisely, a sensitivity grid of
control rates spanning 0.04 to 0.10 is also run.

**Treatment arm (urgent ERCP, within 24 hours).** Rather than assume the anchoring trial's point
estimate, the treatment rate is varied across a grid spanning:

- the null, treatment rate equal to control rate, used to establish type I error;
- the anchoring trial's observed 0.0395;
- relative risk reductions from 0 to 50 percent of the control rate in steps, so that the
  sample-size-versus-effect relationship is characterised rather than assumed at one point.

**Covariate-bearing cohorts (A3 only).** For the prognostic-adjustment aim, patients additionally
carry correlated baseline covariates generated by a Gaussian copula from specified marginals, with
the outcome generated from a logistic model on those covariates whose intercept is calibrated so the
marginal control event rate matches the target. A prognostic model is trained on an independent
simulated historical control cohort, never on the trial data it is later used to analyse.

**Number of repetitions.** 10,000 per scenario for the design-comparison aims. At a rejection
probability near 0.90 this gives a Monte Carlo standard error of
sqrt(0.90 x 0.10 / 10000) = 0.003, and at 0.025 it gives 0.0016, both small relative to the
differences between designs that this study aims to detect. The prognostic-adjustment aim uses 2,000
repetitions per scenario because each replicate additionally fits two generalised linear models; the
resulting Monte Carlo standard error near 0.9 is 0.0067, which remains adequate for the comparison
being made. Monte Carlo standard errors are reported alongside every estimate rather than left
implicit.

**Random number generation.** L'Ecuyer-CMRG streams with one independent substream per scenario, so
results are reproducible and independent of the number of parallel workers. Seeds are recorded in
the results files.

## E — Estimands

Following ICH E9(R1), the primary estimand has five attributes:

- **Population.** Adults with acute cholangitis meeting the trial's eligibility criteria. The
  anchoring trial enrolled mild-to-moderate cholangitis specifically; severe cholangitis, where
  equipoise about delaying drainage is weaker, is outside this estimand and results should not be
  extrapolated to it.
- **Treatment.** Urgent ERCP within 24 hours of presentation, compared with early ERCP at 24 to 48
  hours.
- **Endpoint.** All-cause mortality within 30 days of randomisation.
- **Population-level summary.** The risk difference in 30-day mortality between arms. The odds ratio
  and relative risk are reported as secondary summaries.
- **Intercurrent-event strategy.** Treatment policy. Crossover to earlier ERCP for clinical
  deterioration, and rescue percutaneous or surgical drainage, are expected intercurrent events;
  patients are analysed as randomised regardless of whether they occur. This is the strategy that
  answers the pragmatic question a clinician actually faces at the point of the timing decision.

A secondary safety estimand uses the same attributes with procedure-related adverse events as the
endpoint. This matters because it is the only endpoint on which the anchoring trial found a
significant difference, and it favoured the control arm (17.1 versus 9.2 percent, RR 2.03, 95% CI
1.02 to 4.07). A design powered only on mortality benefit while ignoring procedural harm would
misrepresent the decision.

## M — Methods

Three designs are compared. All use a one-sided type I error rate of 0.025 and target 90 percent
power at the design alternative unless stated otherwise.

1. **Fixed design.** Sample size from `rpact::getSampleSizeRates`. No interim analysis.
2. **Group-sequential design.** Three analyses at equally spaced information fractions, O'Brien-
   Fleming-type alpha spending, with a non-binding O'Brien-Fleming-type futility boundary. Boundaries
   and sample size from `rpact::getDesignGroupSequential` and `rpact::getSampleSizeRates`.
   Boundaries are cross-checked against `gsDesign::gsDesign`.
3. **Bayesian adaptive design.** Independent Beta(1,1) priors on each arm's event rate; conjugate
   Beta posteriors; three analyses; stop for efficacy when the posterior probability that urgent
   ERCP is superior exceeds a threshold, stop for futility when it falls below 0.10. The efficacy
   threshold is calibrated by simulation so that the type I error rate under the null matches 0.025,
   because this design has no closed-form operating characteristics. This is the pattern the FDA's
   Complex Innovative Trial Design programme contemplates: designs whose operating characteristics
   must be established by simulation.

For aim A3, each simulated trial is analysed twice on the same data: unadjusted, and adjusted for a
pre-specified prognostic score as a covariate in a logistic model.

**Validation.** No design quantity in this study is computed by hand where an established engine
provides it. Simulated power at the fixed design's sample size is checked against rpact's analytic
power; group-sequential boundaries are checked against gsDesign; the conjugate posterior probability
is checked against exact numerical integration. These checks are reported with the results, not
assumed.

## P — Performance measures

Reported for every scenario, each with its Monte Carlo standard error:

| Measure | Definition | Monte Carlo SE |
|---|---|---|
| Rejection rate | Proportion of replicates rejecting the null; power under the alternative, type I error under the null | sqrt(p(1-p)/n_sim) |
| Bias | Mean estimated risk difference minus true risk difference | empirical SD / sqrt(n_sim) |
| Empirical SE | SD of the estimated risk difference across replicates | empSE / sqrt(2(n_sim - 1)) |
| Mean squared error | Mean squared deviation of the estimate from the truth | SD of squared errors / sqrt(n_sim) |
| Coverage | Proportion of nominal 95 percent intervals containing the truth | sqrt(p(1-p)/n_sim) |
| Expected sample size | Mean realised total sample size across replicates | SD / sqrt(n_sim) |
| Stopping probabilities | Probability of stopping at each interim, separately for efficacy and futility | sqrt(p(1-p)/n_sim) |

For aim A3 additionally: the ratio of squared standard errors between adjusted and unadjusted
analyses (the variance reduction factor), the implied sample-size reduction, and the type I error
rate of the adjusted analysis under the null.

For aim A4: expected value of sample information across trial sizes, with its Monte Carlo standard
error, and the trial size at which the information gain saturates.

## Deviations from this protocol

Any departure from the above will be recorded in `REPORT.md` with its reason. This section exists so
